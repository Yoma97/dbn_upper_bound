## New structural reduction

For the heat-weighted Riemann kernel

\[
\psi_t(u)=e^{tu^2}\Phi(u),
\]

define

\[
K_{0,t}(r)=\int_{\mathbb R}\psi_t(s+r)\psi_t(s-r)\,ds
\]

and

\[
K_{1,t}(r)=\int_{\mathbb R}\psi_t(s+r)\psi_t(s-r)s^2\,ds.
\]

With `g_t(u)=u psi_t(u)` and its symmetric autocorrelation `C_{g,t}`,

\[
\boxed{K_{1,t}=C_{g,t}+r^2K_{0,t}.}
\]

Both `K_{0,t}` and `C_{g,t}` are automatically positive definite because they are autocorrelations. In Fourier variables,

\[
\widehat K_{0,t}(2x)=2H_t(x)^2,
\qquad
\widehat C_{g,t}(2x)=2H_t'(x)^2,
\]

while

\[
\boxed{\widehat K_{1,t}(2x)=L_1(H_t)(x)=(H_t')^2-H_tH_t''.}
\]

Thus the entire first-Laguerre obstruction is isolated in the Fourier curvature generated by the factor `r^2 K_{0,t}`.

The exact heat-time identity

\[
\boxed{\partial_tK_{0,t}=2K_{1,t}+2r^2K_{0,t}}
\]

provides strong pointwise growth but does not transfer to a Fourier sign because Fourier transform is not order preserving for arbitrary pointwise inequalities.

## Acceptance rule for the theta-series target

An exact theta/Poisson decomposition of `K_{1,t}` counts as progress only if it proves at least one of:

1. a decomposition into positive-definite summands;
2. an independent bound on the Fourier-curvature defect of `K_{0,t}`;
3. a manifestly signed main term plus a rigorously smaller defect controlled by theta/arithmetic structure.

A decomposition into merely pointwise-positive terms is **rejected**: `K_{1,t}>0` is already immediate from its defining integral and does not imply positive definiteness.
