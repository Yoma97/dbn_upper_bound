Connes–Consani–Moscovici identify two essential missing steps in their spectral program:

## 2. PROVED ABSTRACT LEMMA: coercive residual control

Let `A` be a selfadjoint operator with discrete lower spectrum on a Hilbert space `H`. Let `v` be a unit vector in `Dom(A)`, set

    mu = <Av,v>,
    r = (I-|v><v|)Av,
    epsilon = ||r||.

Assume that for some `Delta>0`,

    <Aw,w> >= (mu+Delta)||w||^2

for every `w` in `Dom(A)` with `<w,v>=0`.

Then:

1. `A` has a unique lowest eigenvalue `E0` below `mu+Delta`, hence it is simple;
2. `E0 <= mu` and

       0 <= mu-E0 <= epsilon^2/Delta;

3. after choosing the phase of the normalized ground state `u0` so that `<u0,v> > 0`,

       ||u0-v|| <= epsilon/Delta;

4. if `A` commutes with an involution `J`, and `Jv=v`, then `Ju0=u0`.

### Proof

Decompose `H = Cv direct_sum v^perp`. Relative to this decomposition,

    A = [[mu, r*], [r, B]],

where the quadratic-form hypothesis gives `B >= mu+Delta`.

The Rayleigh quotient of `v` is `mu`, while every vector in `v^perp` has Rayleigh quotient at least `mu+Delta`. By the min–max principle the bottom eigenvalue lies at or below `mu` and the second spectral value is at least `mu+Delta`; hence the ground state is unique.

Writing the ground vector as `a(v+w)`, `w perp v`, its eigenvalue equation gives

    w = -(B-E0)^(-1) r

and

    E0 = mu - <r,(B-E0)^(-1)r>.

Set `d=mu-E0 >= 0`. Since

    B-E0 >= Delta+d,

we obtain

    d <= epsilon^2/(Delta+d) <= epsilon^2/Delta,

and

    ||w|| <= epsilon/(Delta+d) <= epsilon/Delta.

Normalization yields `||u0-v|| <= ||w||`, after phase choice. If `A` and the projection onto `Cv` commute with `J`, then `r`, `B`, and `w` are `J`-even; equivalently uniqueness of the ground eigenspace plus nonzero overlap with the even vector `v` forces the ground state to be even.

This lemma is standard spectral-perturbation/Feshbach machinery in substance; its novelty is NOT claimed. Its role here is to isolate the exact arithmetic estimate required by the RH spectral program.

## 3. CANDIDATE: Prolate–Weil Coercive Gap Theorem

Let `A_lambda` be the selfadjoint operator associated to the semilocal Weil quadratic form `QW_lambda`, and let `k_lambda` be the prolate-based trial vector constructed from the special linear combination of the `h_{0,lambda}` and `h_{4,lambda}` modes. Choose a canonical unit normalization

    v_lambda = k_lambda/||k_lambda||,

and write

    mu_lambda = <A_lambda v_lambda,v_lambda>,
    epsilon_lambda = ||(A_lambda-mu_lambda)v_lambda||.

Seek a positive quantity `Delta_lambda` such that

    <A_lambda w,w>
      >= (mu_lambda+Delta_lambda)||w||^2,
      w perp v_lambda,

and, quantitatively,

    epsilon_lambda/Delta_lambda
      = o(lambda^(-eta))

for every fixed `eta<1/2`.

If these estimates hold, the abstract lemma gives a unique even ground state `xi_lambda` satisfying

    ||xi_lambda-c_lambda k_lambda||_2
      = o(lambda^(-eta))

for every fixed `eta<1/2`, after scalar normalization.

---

## 4. Why this rate is sufficient

For a function `f` supported in `[lambda^(-1),lambda]`, with the Mellin/Fourier transform convention used in the spectral construction, Cauchy–Schwarz gives on every closed substrip `|Im z|<=eta<1/2`

    |f_hat(z)|
      <= ||f||_2
         ( integral_{lambda^-1}^{lambda} u^(2 Im z) d*u )^(1/2)
      <= C_eta lambda^eta ||f||_2.

Therefore

    ||xi_lambda-c_lambda k_lambda||_2
       = o(lambda^(-eta))

implies uniform transform closeness on that substrip.

The cited spectral work already proves that the transform of the canonically normalized `k_lambda` converges to `Xi` on closed substrips of `|Im z|<1/2`. Thus, after compatible scalar normalization, the same would hold for the true lowest Weil eigenfunction.

Every nontrivial zeta zero maps to a zero of `Xi(z)=xi(1/2+iz)` inside the open strip `|Im z|<1/2`. Hence locally uniform convergence there is enough: if every finite spectral transform has only real zeros, Hurwitz excludes a nonreal zero of `Xi` in that strip.

This implication is CONDITIONAL on the Prolate–Weil coercive gap estimates; it is not a proof of RH.

## 5. More realistic refinement: two-mode Feshbach reduction

A one-vector gap may be unnecessarily rigid because `k_lambda` is built from two prolate modes. Let

    V_lambda = span{ E(h_{0,lambda}), E(h_{4,lambda}) }.

A more realistic target is:

1. **High-mode coercivity:** on `V_lambda^perp`,

       QW_lambda[w]
       >= (m_lambda+Delta_lambda)||w||^2;

2. **Small low/high coupling:**

       ||P_{V^perp} A_lambda P_V|| <= epsilon_lambda;

3. **Two-by-two selection:** the effective matrix

       M_lambda=P_V A_lambda P_V

   has a simple lowest eigenvector `v_lambda^(0)` approaching the unique vanishing-integral combination defining `k_lambda`;

4. **Quantitative separation:** the low-mode internal gap and the high-mode gap dominate the coupling strongly enough to give

       dist(xi_lambda, C k_lambda)
       = o(lambda^(-eta))

   for every fixed `eta<1/2`.

This version is a natural Schur/Feshbach problem and matches the actual two-mode origin of the trial state better than an a priori one-dimensional coercivity assumption.

## 6. Fast falsification tests

The candidate must be rejected or weakened if any of the following occurs:

- the second Weil eigenvalue approaches the first at a rate comparable to or faster than the residual, destroying the gap;
- the low/high coupling is not small in the required norm;
- the effective 2x2 ground direction does not converge to the vanishing-integral prolate combination;
- the ratio `epsilon_lambda/Delta_lambda` decays slower than the transform amplification `lambda^eta` near `eta=1/2`;
- a hidden use of Weil positivity/RH is needed to establish the coercivity estimate.

Numerical spectral separation may guide the search but cannot establish any of these uniform asymptotic statements.

## 7. Current smallest missing theorem

The current highest-priority candidate is therefore not “the zeros converge” and not “the lowest eigenvector is prolate”. It is the quantitative statement:

> **Two-mode Prolate–Weil Gap Principle.** The semilocal Weil form has a uniformly isolated low-energy sector generated asymptotically by the two distinguished prolate modes; coupling to its orthogonal complement is smaller than the spectral separation, and the effective 2x2 lowest direction is the vanishing-integral combination at a rate sufficient to survive Mellin-transform amplification on every closed critical substrip.

The next research task is to derive or refute the three required estimates directly from the exact decomposition of `QW_lambda` into its archimedean term, pole/evaluation term, and finite prime-shift operators.
