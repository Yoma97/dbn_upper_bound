## 0. Executive verdict

Rounds 49--50 show that pair correlation has the correct horizontal amplifier but current scalar positivity mechanisms either lack range or suppress the amplifier. This round isolates the ideal spectral defect one would like the explicit formula to measure.

For a finite zero window, there is an exact generating-function identity whose second derivative at the origin is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V(T)
:=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}m_\rho
\left(\beta-\frac12\right)^2\ge0.}
\]

This quantity detects **every** off-line zero, including a single sparse orbit.

However, constructing it by the ordinary one-variable Guinand--Weil calculus would require an exact horizontal projection

\[
\gamma-i\delta\longmapsto\gamma,
\]

and no nonconstant holomorphic test function can implement such a projection on an open strip. Therefore the desired sparse defect is intrinsically non-holomorphic / Hermitian / two-variable from the explicit-formula point of view.

This identifies a genuinely new-tool requirement: an explicit formula for a positive Hermitian spectral functional, not another scalar holomorphic test function.

## 1. Finite-height horizontal generating functions

Write every upper-half-plane zero as

\[
\rho=\frac12+\delta+i\gamma,
\qquad -\frac12<\delta<\frac12,
\qquad 0<\gamma\le T,
\]

and count zeros with multiplicity.

Define

\[
\boxed{
A_T(y)
:=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}
 e^{(\rho-1/2)y}
=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}
 e^{\delta y}e^{i\gamma y}.}
\]

Define the horizontally projected ordinate sum

\[
\boxed{
B_T(y)
:=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}e^{i\gamma y},}
\]

where each zero is still counted with its multiplicity; only its horizontal displacement is erased.

Both sums are finite and entire in `y`.

---

## 2. Functional-equation grouping

For each zero with displacement `delta`, the functional equation supplies a zero with displacement `-delta` at the same positive ordinate. Group the multiset at a fixed ordinate into critical zeros (`delta=0`) and pairs `+-delta`.

Critical zeros cancel identically from `A_T-B_T`. A noncritical pair of common multiplicity `m` contributes

\[
 m\left(e^{\delta y}+e^{-\delta y}-2\right)e^{i\gamma y}
=2m\bigl(\cosh(\delta y)-1\bigr)e^{i\gamma y}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
A_T(y)-B_T(y)
=\sum_{\gamma}
 e^{i\gamma y}
\sum_{\delta>0}
2m_{\gamma,\delta}
\bigl(\cosh(\delta y)-1\bigr).}
\]

The inner coefficient is nonnegative for every real `y`.

---

## 3. Exact horizontal variance

Since

\[
\cosh(\delta y)-1
=\frac12\delta^2y^2+O(y^4),
\]

and the factor `e^{i gamma y}` is multiplied by a function vanishing to second order, the derivatives of the vertical phase do not enter at order two.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(A_T-B_T)(0)=0,}
\]

\[
\boxed{
(A_T-B_T)'(0)=0,}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
(A_T-B_T)''(0)
=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}
 m_\rho\left(\beta-\frac12\right)^2
=:\mathcal V(T).}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\mathcal V(T)\ge0}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V(T)=0
\iff
\beta=\frac12
\text{ for every zero with }0<\gamma\le T.}
\]

This is sparse-complete at finite height: one off-line orbit contributes a strictly positive amount `2m delta^2`.

## 4. Why the first generating function is compatible with analytic spectral tests

Introduce the standard spectral variable

\[
z:=\frac{\rho-1/2}{i}=\gamma-i\delta.
\]

Then

\[
e^{(\rho-1/2)y}=e^{iyz}.
\]

Thus `A_T(y)` is generated by the holomorphic test

\[
h_y(z)=e^{iyz}.
\]

After standard smoothing/truncation, sums of this analytic type lie in the natural orbit of Landau/Guinand--Weil explicit formulas.

The difficulty is not `A_T`; it is the projected sum `B_T`.

## 5. Holomorphic horizontal projection no-go

To obtain `B_T(y)` from the same one-variable calculus by a universal test function, one would need a holomorphic function `H_y(z)` on an open strip containing the possible zero parameters such that

\[
\boxed{
H_y(x+iv)=e^{iyx}}
\]

for all real `x` in an interval and all `v` in an interval.

### Theorem 51.1

If `H` is holomorphic on a connected open set containing a rectangle and depends only on `Re z` throughout that rectangle, then `H` is constant.

### Proof

Write

\[
H(x+iv)=U(x,v)+iV(x,v).
\]

Independence of `v` gives

\[
U_v=V_v=0.
\]

The Cauchy--Riemann equations imply

\[
U_x=V_v=0,
\qquad
V_x=-U_v=0.
\]

Hence both real and imaginary parts are constant on the rectangle, and by the identity theorem `H` is constant on the connected domain. ∎

Therefore, for `y!=0`, no holomorphic universal test can satisfy

\[
H_y(\gamma-i\delta)=e^{iy\gamma}
\]

throughout a two-dimensional strip of possible zero locations.

## 6. Circularity audit

For a **known finite discrete zero set**, one can of course interpolate prescribed values by an entire function. That observation is useless here: constructing the interpolant requires the very off-line zero locations one is trying to exclude, and its prime-side transform would have no uniform a priori control.

The no-go concerns a structural test family chosen independently of the unknown zero configuration.

Thus the following proposal is rejected as circular/non-uniform:

> choose a holomorphic test that maps every actual zero to its vertical projection.

Unless such a test is constructed from independently known arithmetic data with uniform norm control, it simply encodes the unknown zeros.

## 7. What kind of explicit formula is actually needed

The ideal defect

\[
\mathcal V(T)=\sum(\beta-1/2)^2
\]

is a function of both a spectral point and its conjugate / functional-equation partner. It is naturally **Hermitian**, not holomorphic.

A suitable new formula would evaluate a functional of schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_\rho \Psi(z_\rho,\overline{z_\rho})}
\]

or, more realistically, a positive quadratic form

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\rho,\rho'}
\Psi(z_\rho,\overline{z_{\rho'}})}
\]

with a prime-side representation and a norm/error that can be controlled unconditionally.

Pair correlation already has this two-variable/Hermitian character, but Rounds 49--50 show that the existing scalar pair kernels do not yet provide sparse-complete localization.

## 8. New mathematical tool specification

### HEPF — Hermitian Explicit Projection Formula

Seek a class of kernels `Psi(z,wbar)` satisfying:

1. **positive defect:**
   \[
   \Psi(z,\bar z)\ge c\,(\Im z)^2
   \]
   or an amplified variant, with equality on the real spectral axis;
2. **Gram positivity:** the full double sum is a norm/sum of squares, so one orbit cannot be cancelled arbitrarily;
3. **prime-side computability:** the quadratic form has an explicit arithmetic representation involving primes/prime powers or Dirichlet polynomials;
4. **uniformity:** kernel construction is independent of the unknown zero set;
5. **sparse amplification:** a single off-real spectral point eventually exceeds the arithmetic error budget.

This is strictly more specific than asking for a new Weil positivity criterion: the desired object must resolve horizontal displacement quantitatively and have a non-RH source of positivity.
