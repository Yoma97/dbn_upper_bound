## 2. PROVED ABSTRACT LEMMA: radical truncation identity

Let `W` be a Hermitian sesquilinear form on a vector space/domain and let `g` lie in its radical:

    W(g,h)=0

for every admissible `h`. Let `R` be any linear truncation/projection for which all terms below are in the form domain, and write

    g_R = Rg,
    tau = (I-R)g.

Then

    W(g_R,g_R) = W(tau,tau).

More generally, if `g=E f` and `h=E q` are both in the radical,

    W(Rg,Rh) = W((I-R)g,(I-R)h).

### Proof

Since `g_R=g-tau`, expansion gives

    W(g_R,g_R)
      = W(g,g)-W(g,tau)-W(tau,g)+W(tau,tau)
      = W(tau,tau).

The bilinear version is identical.

Thus a semilocal Weil value on a truncated global-radical vector is exactly a boundary/tail defect; it is not an independent bulk contribution.

## 3. PROVED: Poisson converts the lower multiplicative tail into Fourier leakage

Use the arithmetic summation map

    E(f)(u) = u^(1/2) sum_{n>=1} f(nu),   u>0,

on an even class for which the Poisson relation

    E(Fourier(f))(u) = E(f)(u^(-1))

holds (in the standard setup one imposes the required cancellation conditions such as `f(0)=Fourier(f)(0)=0`).

Assume `f` is supported in `[-lambda,lambda]`, and let `P_lambda` denote this cutoff. Put

    h = (I-P_lambda) Fourier(f).

Then:

1. `E(f)(u)=0` for `u>lambda`;
2. for `0<u<lambda^(-1)`,

       E(f)(u) = E(h)(u^(-1)).

### Proof

For `u>lambda`, every `nu>lambda`, so every term in the arithmetic sum vanishes.

For `0<u<lambda^-1`, put `v=u^-1>lambda`. Poisson gives

    E(f)(u)=E(Fourier(f))(v).

Split `Fourier(f)=P_lambda Fourier(f)+h`. The first term has support in `[-lambda,lambda]`, hence its arithmetic sum is zero at `v>lambda`. Therefore

    E(f)(u)=E(h)(v)=E(h)(u^-1).

Consequently, using invariance of `d*u=du/u` under inversion,

    integral_0^(lambda^-1) |E(f)(u)|^2 d*u
      = integral_lambda^infinity |E(h)(v)|^2 d*v.

This is an exact localization-to-leakage identity.

## 4. PROVED: arithmetic summation does not arbitrarily amplify a weighted Fourier tail

Let `h` be supported on `[lambda,infinity)` (the positive tail; for even functions the negative tail is handled symmetrically). For every `epsilon>0`,

    integral_lambda^infinity |E(h)(v)|^2 d*v
      <= C_epsilon lambda^(-1-epsilon)
         integral_lambda^infinity x^(1+epsilon)|h(x)|^2 dx.

### Proof

Since

    |E(h)(v)|^2 d*v
      = |sum_{n>=1} h(nv)|^2 dv,

weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields

    |sum h(nv)|^2
      <= zeta(1+epsilon)
         sum_{n>=1} n^(1+epsilon)|h(nv)|^2.

Integrating `v>=lambda`, changing variable `x=nv`, and interchanging the positive sum/integral gives

    <= zeta(1+epsilon)
       integral_lambda^infinity |h(x)|^2
       sum_{n<=x/lambda} n^epsilon dx.

Using

    sum_{n<=X} n^epsilon <= C_epsilon X^(1+epsilon)

proves the bound.

Hence the multiplicative lower tail of `E(f)` is quantitatively controlled by a weighted Fourier leakage of `f`.

## 5. Differential version

Let `D=v d/dv` be the dilation derivative. A direct termwise computation gives

    D E(h) = E((x d/dx + 1/2)h),

and therefore

    D^j E(h) = E((x d/dx + 1/2)^j h).

Thus the same arithmetic tail estimate applies to logarithmic derivatives after replacing `h` by the corresponding dilation derivatives. This is the route to Sobolev-type control of the tail needed by the Weil distribution.

## 6. PROVED GENERAL SAMPLING LEMMA (strip version)

Let `F` be a sufficiently smooth function in additive logarithmic coordinates and define a weighted Sobolev-L1 norm

    ||F||_{X_m}
      = sum_{j=0}^m integral_R exp(|x|/2)|F^(j)(x)| dx.

For `m` sufficiently large, the Fourier transform satisfies uniformly for `|Im z|<=1/2`

    |F_hat(z)| <= C_m (1+|Re z|)^(-m) ||F||_{X_m}.

Using the classical zero counting estimate that the number of zeta zeros in each unit-height interval is `O(log(2+|T|))`, it follows that

    sum_rho |F_hat(s_rho)|^2 <= C_m' ||F||_{X_m}^2,

where `rho=1/2+i s_rho` and therefore `|Im s_rho|<1/2` unconditionally.

### Proof sketch

For `z=t+iy`, write the transform as the Fourier transform of `e^{yx}F(x)`. Repeated integration by parts gives polynomial decay in `t`, uniformly for `|y|<=1/2`, controlled by `X_m`. Summing the square over unit-height zero blocks converges once `m` is chosen large enough.

By polarization the global Weil zero-sampling form is therefore continuous on this weighted smooth class without assuming RH.

## 7. New exact bridge

Combining Sections 2–6 gives the structural chain

    Slepian/Fourier leakage of f
      -> multiplicative tail of E(f)
      -> entire semilocal Weil defect of R_lambda E(f).

In particular, for an admissible time-limited constrained prolate state, the semilocal Weil Rayleigh value is not a mysterious bulk arithmetic quantity: it is generated by failure of exact simultaneous time/Fourier localization.

This explains structurally why prolate vectors can create extremely small Weil eigenvalues, without using RH.

## 8. Remaining theorem: weighted leakage-to-spectral-selection

The exact identities above give upper/smallness control, but they do NOT yet prove that the first constrained prolate state is the *lowest* Weil state, nor do they give the required eigenvalue gap.

The next candidate is therefore narrower:

> **Weighted Weil–Slepian Selection Theorem.** On the constrained prolate ladder, the weighted Fourier leakage norms controlling the Weil tail are asymptotically ordered strongly enough, and the off-diagonal Weil tail pairings are sufficiently smaller than the first gap, that the constrained Slepian minimizer selects the unique lowest Weil eigenstate.

To prove it one needs two additional ingredients:

1. weighted derivative-tail estimates for the first constrained prolate modes (the ordinary L2 leakage is not by itself enough for the Weil distribution);
2. a lower/separation estimate preventing another near-radical mode from having an even smaller Weil Rayleigh value through arithmetic cancellation.

The second item is now the main obstruction.
