## 1. Source inputs

We use only the following established facts.

1. The Riemann heat family

\[
H_t(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du
\]

is even, real-entire, of order one, and satisfies

\[
\partial_tH_t=-\partial_z^2H_t.
\]

There is a finite de Bruijn--Newman constant \(\Lambda\) such that all zeros of \(H_t\) are real iff \(t\ge\Lambda\).

2. Polymath Theorem 1.5: there exist absolute constants \(C\) large and \(c>0\) small such that, for every \(0<t\le 1/2\), every zero of \(H_t\) with real part \(x\ge e^{C/t}\) is real and lies in a unique asymptotic zero disk; conversely each sufficiently large asymptotic disk contains precisely one zero (counting multiplicity). Hence these high zeros are simple. Evenness gives the same conclusion for large negative real part.

3. The de Bruijn strip propagation bound, together with the critical-strip location at \(t=0\), bounds the imaginary parts of zeros uniformly on every compact positive time interval (and in particular by 1 for the interval needed below).

4. Csordas records that \(\Phi(u)>0\), \(\Phi'(u)<0\) for \(u>0\), and that

\[
r\longmapsto \log \Phi(\sqrt r)
\]

is strictly concave on \((0,\infty)\).

5. Rodgers--Tao proved \(\Lambda\ge0\).

## 2. Positive-threshold finite-collision theorem

### Theorem 13.1 — threshold collision cannot escape to infinity

**PROVED (conditional only on the established Polymath/de Bruijn properties above).**

Assume \(\Lambda>0\). Then \(H_\Lambda\) has a multiple real zero. More precisely, if \(C\) is the absolute constant in Polymath Theorem 1.5, every multiple zero of \(H_\Lambda\) lies in a finite window; one may take a safe asymptotic cutoff

\[
X_\Lambda=\exp\!\left(\frac{2C}{\Lambda}\right)
\]

and conclude that at least one multiple zero lies in \(|x|<X_\Lambda\).

### Proof

Because \(\Lambda>0\) and the unconditional Polymath upper bound gives \(\Lambda<1/2\), consider

\[
J=[\Lambda/2,\Lambda].
\]

For every \(t\in J\),

\[
e^{C/t}\le e^{2C/\Lambda}=X_\Lambda.
\]

Hence Polymath Theorem 1.5 implies that every zero of \(H_t\) with \(|\Re z|\ge X_\Lambda\) is real and simple, uniformly for \(t\in J\). Thus a loss of real-rootedness as \(t\downarrow\Lambda\) cannot be generated by a collision escaping through infinite real height.

At \(t=\Lambda\), all zeros are real by the defining closed half-line property \(\{t: H_t\text{ is real-rooted}\}=[\Lambda,\infty)\).

Suppose for contradiction that every zero of \(H_\Lambda\) were simple. Choose \(X>X_\Lambda\) so that the vertical sides \(\Re z=\pm X\) contain no zero of \(H_\Lambda\). By the de Bruijn strip bound, all zeros relevant to \(t\in J\) lie in a fixed horizontal strip, so the part with \(|\Re z|\le X\) is compact and contains only finitely many zeros of \(H_\Lambda\).

Surround those finitely many simple real zeros by pairwise disjoint small disks centered on the real axis. Uniform convergence \(H_t\to H_\Lambda\) on compact sets as \(t\to\Lambda\), together with Rouche's theorem, gives for all \(t<\Lambda\) sufficiently close to \(\Lambda\):

- exactly one zero (counting multiplicity) in each disk;
- no zeros in the compact complement of the disks.

Because \(H_t\) is real on the real axis and satisfies conjugation symmetry, the unique zero in a disk symmetric about the real axis must equal its conjugate; therefore it is real. Outside the compact window, Polymath Theorem 1.5 already forces every zero to be real. Consequently all zeros of \(H_t\) would be real for some \(t<\Lambda\), contradicting the definition of \(\Lambda\).

Therefore \(H_\Lambda\) has a multiple real zero, necessarily at finite height. ∎

### Consequence

The Round-12 warning that a hypothetical positive \(\Lambda\) might be produced solely by collisions escaping to height infinity is **REFUTED for the Riemann heat family when \(\Lambda>0\)**. It remains a legitimate pathology for more general infinite entire heat families, but Polymath asymptotics exclude it here.

Thus C6 does **not** require a priori a height-uniform bound on the rootwise quantity \(R_k\) all the way to infinity. If \(\Lambda>0\), the obstruction is attained by an actual finite collision at \(t=\Lambda\).

## 4. Universal Hermite collision residue

Let \(F_t\) be a real analytic backward-heat family,

\[
\partial_tF=-F_{xx},
\]

and suppose that at \((t_c,x_c)\), \(F_{t_c}\) has a zero of exact multiplicity \(m\ge2\):

\[
F_{t_c}(x_c+z)=a z^m+O(z^{m+1}),\qquad a\ne0.
\]

Put \(\tau=t-t_c>0\), on the real-rooted side of the collision. Parabolic Taylor expansion and the heat equation give

\[
F_{t_c+\tau}(x_c+\sqrt\tau X)
=a\tau^{m/2}Q_m(X)+O(\tau^{(m+1)/2}),
\]

where

\[
Q_m(X)=e^{-D_X^2}X^m=H_m(X/2)
\]

with \(H_m\) the physicists' Hermite polynomial. Hence \(Q_m\) has \(m\) simple real roots \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_m\), and the local root branches satisfy

\[
x_k(t)=x_c+\sqrt\tau\,\xi_k+O(\tau).
\]

Moreover,

\[
F_x(t,x_k(t))
=a\tau^{(m-1)/2}Q_m'(\xi_k)\bigl(1+O(\sqrt\tau)\bigr).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{dt}\log|F_x(t,x_k(t))|
=\frac{m-1}{2(t-t_c)}+O((t-t_c)^{-1/2}).
}
\]

Using the exact Round-12 identity

\[
R_k(t)=
\frac14(\partial_x\log L_1)^2+3\frac{L_2}{L_1}
=\frac d{dt}\log|F_x(t,x_k(t))|,
\]

we obtain the universal collision residue

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{t\downarrow t_c}(t-t_c)R_k(t)=\frac{m-1}{2}.
}
\]

**Status:** PROVED local theorem. **NOVELTY UNVERIFIED.**

### Hermite gap identity

For any root \(\xi_k\) of \(Q_m\), write

\[
A_k^{(m)}=\sum_{j\ne k}\frac1{\xi_k-\xi_j},\qquad
B_k^{(m)}=\sum_{j\ne k}\frac1{(\xi_k-\xi_j)^2}.
\]

Then exact scaling gives

\[
\boxed{
\left(A_k^{(m)}\right)^2+3B_k^{(m)}=\frac{m-1}{2}.
}
\]

This can also be checked directly from the Hermite differential equation for \(Q_m\).

Examples:

- \(m=2\): \(Q_2=X^2-2\), residue \(1/2\).
- \(m=3\): \(Q_3=X^3-6X\), residue \(1\).

This fixes the precise singular signature C6 must exclude at a positive threshold.

## 5. New Riemann-kernel collision pair

Define

\[
\psi_t(u)=e^{tu^2}\Phi(u),\qquad
w_t(u)=u\psi_t(u)>0\quad(u>0),
\]

and

\[
q(u)=-\frac{\Phi'(u)}{u\Phi(u)}.
\]

### Lemma 13.2 — q is strictly increasing

**PROVED from Csordas' established concavity theorem.**

Set

\[
f(r)=\log\Phi(\sqrt r).
\]

Csordas records \(f''(r)<0\) for \(r>0\). Since

\[
f'(u^2)=\frac{\Phi'(u)}{2u\Phi(u)},
\]

we have

\[
q(u)=-2f'(u^2),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{q'(u)=-4u f''(u^2)>0\qquad(u>0).}
\]

The key point is that **q is independent of the heat time t**.

### Lemma 13.3 — a collision is a common sine-transform zero for a monotone likelihood-ratio pair

Define

\[
S_0(t,x)=\int_0^\infty w_t(u)\sin(xu)\,du=-H_t'(x),
\]

and

\[
S_1(t,x)=\int_0^\infty q(u)w_t(u)\sin(xu)\,du.
\]

Because \(q(u)w_t(u)=-e^{tu^2}\Phi'(u)\), integration by parts gives

\[
\boxed{S_1(t,x)=xH_t(x)-2tH_t'(x).}
\]

Consequently, for \(x>0\),

\[
\boxed{
H_t(x)=H_t'(x)=0
\iff S_0(t,x)=S_1(t,x)=0.
}
\]

Both sine-transform kernels are strictly positive on \((0,\infty)\), and their ratio \(q\) is strictly increasing and independent of \(t\).

This is a genuinely kernel-side interface different from merely writing \(J=-L_1\), although the common-zero statement remains RH-equivalent once quantified over all \(t>0\).

## 6. A rigorous necessary overshoot condition for any collision

For \(x>0\), define the cumulative sine balance

\[
M_{t,x}(v)=\int_0^v w_t(u)\sin(xu)\,du.
\]

At a collision, \(S_0=0\), hence

\[
M_{t,x}(0)=M_{t,x}(\infty)=0.
\]

Also \(S_1=0\). Since \(dM=w_t(u)\sin(xu)du\), integration by parts gives

\[
0=\int_0^\infty q\,dM
=-\int_0^\infty q'(u)M_{t,x}(u)\,du,
\]

where the boundary term vanishes by the super-exponential decay of \(\Phi\) and its derivatives.

Because \(q'(u)>0\), while \(M_{t,x}(v)>0\) for all sufficiently small \(v>0\), a collision forces

\[
\boxed{
\exists v>0:\quad M_{t,x}(v)<0.
}
\]

Thus every positive-time collision requires a genuine **cumulative sine overshoot**: the first positive balance must later cross below zero before returning to zero at infinity.

**Status:** PROVED necessary condition. It is not yet a no-collision theorem.

## 7. The smallest non-circular next lemma

The previous section isolates a sharply falsifiable kernel theorem:

### CANDIDATE — Cumulative Sine-Balance Lemma

For the specific Riemann kernel and \(0<t\le1/2\), prove that whenever

\[
S_0(t,x)=\int_0^\infty u e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du=0,
\]

one has

\[
M_{t,x}(v)=\int_0^v u e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\sin(xu)\,du\ge0
\quad\text{for every }v\ge0,
\]

with strict positivity for some interval.

If true, Lemma 13.3 and the overshoot identity would exclude every positive-time collision and hence, by Theorem 13.1 plus Rodgers--Tao, imply RH.

### Why this is not accepted yet

- Strict log-concavity alone does **not** automatically imply this oscillatory cumulative inequality.
- The sine kernel is not globally totally positive, so a generic monotone-likelihood-ratio argument is insufficient.
- Therefore the lemma must be proved from additional special structure of \(\Phi\), or it must be refuted quickly by a counterexample/numerical test.

### Preferred next attack

Work in the variable \(r=u^2\):

\[
S_0(t,x)=\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{tr}\Phi(\sqrt r)\sin(x\sqrt r)\,dr.
\]

The density

\[
K_t(r)=e^{tr}\Phi(\sqrt r)
\]

has

\[
\log K_t(r)=tr+\log\Phi(\sqrt r),
\]

so it remains strictly log-concave for every real \(t\). This preservation is an independent, established kernel property and is the strongest currently identified structural input for attacking the cumulative balance inequality.

The first task is **falsification**, not proof by wishful analogy: test the cumulative sign at critical points \(H_t'(x)=0\) for several \(t\), especially near the known upper-bound regime and in the intermediate-height transition zone. If a negative cumulative balance occurs while \(H_t' = 0\), reject this lemma immediately.
