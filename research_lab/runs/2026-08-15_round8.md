## 1. PROVED: all-orders arithmetic boundary-symbol expansion

Use the Fourier convention

    fhat(xi)=int_R f(x)e^{-2 pi i x xi} dx.

Let `f` be even and `C^{M+1}` on `[-L,L]`, extended by zero outside. Repeated integration by parts gives

    fhat(xi)
      = sum_{j=0}^{M}
          [ f^(j)(-L)e^(2 pi i L xi)
            - f^(j)(L)e^(-2 pi i L xi) ]
          /(2 pi i xi)^(j+1)
        + Rhat_{M+1}(xi),

with

    |Rhat_{M+1}(xi)|
      <= ||f^(M+1)||_1 /(2 pi |xi|)^(M+1).

For even `f`, `f^(j)(-L)=(-1)^j f^(j)(L)`. For `v>L`, summing over `xi=nv` and using the Fourier series of periodic Bernoulli polynomials yields

    E(fhat)(v)
      = sum_{k=1}^{M}
          (-1)^k f^(k-1)(L) B_k({Lv})
          /[ k! v^(k-1/2) ]
        + R_M(v),

where `{x}` is the fractional part (with the usual midpoint convention for `B_1` at integers), and

    |R_M(v)|
      <= C_M v^(-M-1/2)
         [ |f^(M)(L)| + ||f^(M+1)||_1 ].

### Proof

For `k=j+1`, the positive-frequency boundary sum is

    sum_{n>=1}
      [(-1)^(k-1)e^(2 pi i nLv)-e^(-2 pi i nLv)]/n^k.

This is `(-1)^(k-1)` times the full nonzero-integer Fourier series for `B_k`, namely

    sum_{n!=0} e^(2 pi i n t)/n^k
      = -(2 pi i)^k B_k({t})/k!.

The `k=1` identity is interpreted as the bounded sawtooth Fourier series; for `k>=2` convergence is absolute. The remainder is absolutely summable because the retained remainder has power at least `n^(-M-1)`.

This is an exact arithmetic boundary-layer expansion generated by time truncation.

## 2. Poisson form of the lower multiplicative boundary layer

On the standard Poisson-cancellation class,

    E(f)(u)=E(fhat)(u^-1).

Thus for `0<u<L^-1`,

    E(f)(u)
      = sum_{k=1}^{M}
          (-1)^k f^(k-1)(L) B_k({L/u})
          u^(k-1/2)/k!
        + O( u^(M+1/2)
             [|f^(M)(L)|+||f^(M+1)||_1] ).

The lower multiplicative tail is therefore a rapidly oscillating boundary layer whose amplitudes are exactly the endpoint jets of the time-limited state.

## 3. PROVED: periodic weighted integral lemma

Let `P` be bounded and 1-periodic with mean `Pbar`, and assume the primitive of `P-Pbar` is bounded (true for products of periodic Bernoulli polynomials). For `p>1`,

    int_A^infinity P(t)t^(-p) dt
      = Pbar A^(1-p)/(p-1) + O_P(A^(-p)).

### Proof

Write `P=Pbar+Q'` with bounded periodic primitive `Q`, integrate the zero-mean part by parts, and use `p>1`.

## 4. PROVED: leading L2 arithmetic-tail energy and jet hierarchy

For the boundary-symbol terms of orders `k,l`, the multiplicative L2 pairing contains

    int_L^infinity
      B_k({Lv}) B_l({Lv}) v^(-k-l) dv.

Changing variable `t=Lv` and applying the periodic lemma gives

    = <B_k,B_l>_[0,1]
      /[(k+l-1)L^(k+l-1)]
      + O(L^(-k-l)),

where

    <B_k,B_l> = int_0^1 B_k(x)B_l(x) dx.

The Bernoulli Gram coefficients are explicit; for `k,l>=1`,

    int_0^1 B_k(x)B_l(x) dx
      = (-1)^(k-1) k! l! B_{k+l}/(k+l)!

when interpreted with the standard Bernoulli convention (and they vanish when `k+l` is odd and greater than 1).

Hence, whenever the remainder is uniform enough relative to the endpoint jets,

    ||1_(L,infinity) E(fhat)||^2_{L2(d*v)}

has an asymptotic expansion as an explicit positive Gram form in the scaled boundary jets.

In particular:

- if `f(L) != 0`, the leading term is

      |f(L)|^2 /(12 L) + lower order;

  because `int_0^1 B_1^2=1/12`;

- if `f(L)=0` but `f'(L)!=0`, the first surviving term is

      |f'(L)|^2 /(2160 L^3) + lower order,

  because `int_0^1 B_2^2=1/180` and the coefficient includes `(2!)^-2` and `(2+2-1)^-1`.

More generally, if

    f(L)=f'(L)=...=f^(r-1)(L)=0,
    f^r(L)!=0,

then the first possible diagonal tail energy occurs at order

    L^(-(2r+1)).

Thus each additional endpoint-jet cancellation gains two powers of the multiplicative window scale in the arithmetic L2 defect.

## 6. Destroyer consequence: scalar leakage ordering is structurally incomplete

The leading boundary energy is a Gram form in endpoint evaluations. On a multi-mode prolate sector its matrix is rank one at the first jet level:

    [ f_j(L) conjugate(f_k(L)) ]_{j,k}.

Therefore linear combinations can cancel the leading boundary defect even if every individual Slepian leakage is nonzero. On the kernel of this first boundary trace, the next Bernoulli/jet matrix becomes visible, and so on.

This creates a **boundary-jet filtration** rather than a scalar eigenvalue ordering. It explains why a growing near-radical sector cannot be reduced safely to a single concentration eigenvalue.
