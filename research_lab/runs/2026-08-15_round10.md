## 0. First-principles setting

Let a real entire heat family satisfy

    partial_t F_t(z) = - partial_z^2 F_t(z).

For a real entire function F define generalized Laguerre coefficients by

    |F(x+i y)|^2 = sum_{n>=0} L_n(x;F) y^(2n).

Thus

    L_0 = F^2,
    L_1 = (F')^2 - F F''.

The full inequalities `L_n>=0` characterize Laguerre–Polya membership in the standard setting, and in particular imply real-rootedness. Static `L_1>=0` alone is not sufficient.

Round 1 proved the exact heat hierarchy

    (partial_t + 1/2 partial_x^2) L_n
      = (n+1)(2n+1) L_(n+1).

In particular

    (partial_t + 1/2 partial_x^2) L_1 = 6 L_2.

# 1. DESTROYER: dynamic L1 alone cannot detect every collision multiplicity

The simplest triple-collision model is

    F_epsilon(x) = exp(epsilon D^2) x^3
                 = x^3 + 6 epsilon x,
    epsilon>0.

It has the non-real pair

    x = +/- i sqrt(6 epsilon)

and a real zero at `0`. Nevertheless

    L_1(x;F_epsilon)
      = (3x^2+6epsilon)^2 - (x^3+6epsilon x)(6x)
      = 3x^4 + 36 epsilon^2
      > 0

for every real `x`.

Hence even along a heat unfolding of a triple collision, `L_1` can remain strictly positive. Any attempt to prove RH using only the real-axis inequality `L_1(H_t)>=0` for all positive times is therefore structurally incomplete unless higher multiplicities are independently excluded.

# 2. PROVED: scaled local model of an arbitrary heat collision

Suppose `F_t` is analytic in `(t,z)` near `(t_c,x_c)`, is real on the real axis for real `t`, and satisfies the backward heat equation. Assume `F_(t_c)` has a real zero of exact multiplicity `m>=2` at `x_c`:

    F_(t_c)(x_c+z) = a z^m + O(z^(m+1)),   a != 0.

Put `epsilon=t_c-t>0` and define

    G_epsilon(X)
      = a^(-1) epsilon^(-m/2)
        F_(t_c-epsilon)(x_c + sqrt(epsilon) X).

Then, locally uniformly together with every fixed number of `X`-derivatives,

    G_epsilon(X) -> P_m(X) := exp(D_X^2) X^m

as `epsilon -> 0+`.

### Proof

Because `partial_t F=-D^2F`, moving backward in heat time by `epsilon` gives locally

    F_(t_c-epsilon) = exp(epsilon D^2) F_(t_c).

Apply the operator to the Taylor expansion at `x_c`. The monomial `a z^m` gives exactly

    a epsilon^(m/2) exp(D_X^2) X^m

under `z=sqrt(epsilon)X`. Every Taylor term of degree `m+r`, `r>=1`, contributes `O(epsilon^((m+r)/2))` after the same scaling, locally uniformly with fixed derivatives. Dividing by `a epsilon^(m/2)` proves the claim.

Since `L_n` is quadratic and contains total derivative order `2n`, scaling also gives

    L_n(x_c+sqrt(epsilon)X; F_(t_c-epsilon))
      = a^2 epsilon^(m-n) L_n(X;P_m)
        + o(epsilon^(m-n))

locally uniformly for each fixed `n`.

# 3. PROVED: first two Laguerre coefficients detect every collision multiplicity

The heat polynomial is

    P_m(X)=exp(D^2)X^m
          = sum_{j=0}^{floor(m/2)} m!/[j!(m-2j)!] X^(m-2j).

## 3.1 Even multiplicity m=2k

At `X=0`,

    P_(2k)(0)   = (2k)!/k!,
    P_(2k)'(0)  = 0,
    P_(2k)''(0) = (2k)!/(k-1)!.

Therefore

    L_1(0;P_(2k))
      = - [(2k)!]^2/[k!(k-1)!]
      < 0.

Moreover `P_(2k)(iy)` is a real polynomial in `y^2` with alternating signs and positive coefficient magnitudes. The coefficient of `y^4` in `|P_(2k)(iy)|^2=P_(2k)(iy)^2` is

    P''(0)^2/4 + P(0)P''''(0)/12 > 0

(with the second term absent when `k=1`). Hence

    L_2(0;P_(2k)) > 0.

Thus

    L_1(0;P_(2k)) L_2(0;P_(2k)) < 0.

## 3.2 Odd multiplicity m=2k+1, k>=1

Now

    A := P_(2k+1)'(0)   = (2k+1)!/k!,
    B := P_(2k+1)'''(0) = (2k+1)!/(k-1)!.

Since `P(0)=P''(0)=0`,

    L_1(0;P_(2k+1)) = A^2 > 0.

Writing

    P(iy)=i[A y - B y^3/6 + O(y^5)]

gives

    |P(iy)|^2
      = A^2 y^2 - (A B/3)y^4 + O(y^6),

and therefore

    L_2(0;P_(2k+1))
      = - A B/3
      = - [(2k+1)!]^2/[3 k!(k-1)!]
      < 0.

Again

    L_1(0;P_m)L_2(0;P_m)<0.

## 3.3 Collision conclusion

By the scaling limit of Section 2, for every multiplicity `m>=2`, there exists `epsilon_0>0` such that for `0<epsilon<epsilon_0`,

    L_1(x_c;F_(t_c-epsilon))
    L_2(x_c;F_(t_c-epsilon)) < 0.

This is the **Dynamic Two-Laguerre Collision Lemma**.

Parity of the collision multiplicity selects the detector:

- even multiplicity: `L_1<0<L_2` just below collision;
- odd multiplicity >=3: `L_2<0<L_1` just below collision.

Thus the pair `(L_1,L_2)` detects every finite real multiple-zero heat collision, while `L_1` alone does not.

# 4. PROVED: polynomial Two-Laguerre Heat Criterion

Let `p` be a nonzero real polynomial and

    p_t = exp(-tD^2)p,   t>=0.

Then

    p has only real zeros

if and only if

    L_1(x;p_t)L_2(x;p_t) >= 0

for every real `x` and every `t>=0`.

### Forward implication

The operator `exp(-tD^2)` preserves real-rootedness in this direction. Hence each `p_t` is hyperbolic. Generalized Laguerre inequalities give `L_1,L_2>=0`.

### Converse implication

Assume `p` is not hyperbolic. For sufficiently large `t`, after the standard scaling by `sqrt(t)`, the leading-degree part of `p_t` tends to a Hermite polynomial with simple real zeros, so `p_t` is eventually hyperbolic with simple zeros. Let

    T = inf {tau : p_t is hyperbolic for every t>=tau}.

Then `T>0`. Hyperbolicity is closed under coefficient limits, so `p_T` is hyperbolic. It cannot have only simple zeros, because simple hyperbolicity is open in coefficient space and would contradict minimality of `T`. Therefore `p_T` has a real multiple zero. The collision lemma then gives

    L_1 L_2 < 0

at a nearby time just below `T`, contradicting the assumed sign condition.

So `p` must be hyperbolic.

This theorem is independent of zeta and is a legitimate transfer theorem for heat-evolved real polynomials. Literature novelty is not yet certified.

# 5. PROVED FROM KNOWN INPUTS: Dynamic Two-Laguerre Criterion for RH

Let

    H_t(z)=int_0^infinity exp(tu^2) Phi(u) cos(zu)du,

so that

    partial_t H_t = -partial_z^2 H_t.

Then

    RH

is equivalent to

    L_1(x;H_t)L_2(x;H_t) >= 0

for every real `x` and every `0<t<=1/2`.

## Forward implication

If RH holds, `H_0` has only real zeros. The de Bruijn/Polya heat-preservation theorem then gives real-rootedness of `H_t` for every `t>=0`. Hence all generalized Laguerre expressions, in particular `L_1,L_2`, are nonnegative.

## Converse implication

Assume the displayed two-Laguerre sign condition but suppose RH is false. Then the de Bruijn-Newman constant satisfies `Lambda>0`.

Choose `t_j<Lambda`, `t_j -> Lambda`. Since `t_j<Lambda`, `H_(t_j)` has a non-real zero `z_j`.

For `j` large, `t_j>=Lambda/2`. Polymath Theorem 1.5 gives an absolute `C` such that every zero of `H_(t_j)` with sufficiently large real part

    |Re z| >= exp(C/t_j)

is real. Hence the chosen non-real zeros have

    |Re z_j| <= exp(2C/Lambda)

for all sufficiently large `j` (using even symmetry for negative real parts).

The de Bruijn strip estimate supplies a uniform bound on `|Im z_j|`. Therefore the `z_j` lie in a fixed compact set. Pass to a subsequence:

    z_j -> z_*.

Local uniform dependence of `H_t` on `(t,z)` gives

    H_Lambda(z_*)=0.

At `t=Lambda` all zeros are real, so `z_*=x_*` is real.

If `x_*` were a simple zero of `H_Lambda`, then by Rouché/implicit-function continuation there would be exactly one nearby zero for real `t` close to `Lambda`; reality of the family would force that branch to remain real. This contradicts the non-real zeros `z_j` converging to `x_*`. Hence `x_*` is a real multiple zero of `H_Lambda`.

Apply the Dynamic Two-Laguerre Collision Lemma at `(Lambda,x_*)`. For `t<Lambda` sufficiently close to `Lambda`,

    L_1(x_*;H_t)L_2(x_*;H_t)<0,

contradicting the assumed sign condition. Therefore `Lambda<=0`, hence RH.

### Logical status

This is an RH-equivalent dynamic criterion. It is **not** itself progress toward a proof unless the sign condition can be deduced from an independent structural property of the Riemann kernel.

Its value is that the static infinite Laguerre hierarchy is compressed, using heat dynamics plus the no-escape theorem, to the first two Laguerre coefficients.

# 6. PROVED: single parabolic sign-coherence formulation

Round 1 gave

    (partial_t + 1/2 partial_x^2)L_1 = 6L_2.

Hence the Dynamic Two-Laguerre criterion is equivalent to

    L_1 (partial_t + 1/2 partial_x^2)L_1 >= 0

for all real `x` and positive heat times in the stated range.

Equivalently,

    (partial_t + 1/2 partial_x^2)(L_1^2)
      >= (partial_x L_1)^2.

Call this the **Parabolic Laguerre Sign-Coherence inequality**.

Again, for the Riemann heat family this inequality is RH-equivalent, not an independent proof.

# 7. Why this changes the research obstruction

Static theory says:

    all L_n>=0  <->  Laguerre-Polya / all zeros real

in the applicable entire-function class. That is an infinite endpoint family.

The heat-flow argument says that a *first loss of real-rootedness* is much more rigid: every finite collision is detected by only `L_1` and `L_2`, with parity selecting the sign failure.

Therefore the next non-tautological target is no longer

    prove all generalized Laguerre inequalities.

It is:

> Find an independently provable kernel/PDE/operator theorem for the Riemann heat family that implies Parabolic Laguerre Sign-Coherence.

A useful theorem should apply to a wider class of positive even kernels, should not mention their zero sets, and should not be equivalent to Laguerre-Polya membership by definition.
