\[
|F(x+iy)|^2=\sum_{n\ge0}L_n(x;F)y^{2n},
\]

Therefore merely asking to prove all \(L_n\ge0\) or all \(K_n\) positive definite is an endpoint reformulation, not a new proof mechanism.

# 1. PROVED TOOL CANDIDATE: Laguerre–Bessel transport under backward heat flow

## 1.1 Setting

Let \(F_t(z)\) be a real entire family, sufficiently regular for the differentiations below, satisfying

\[
\partial_tF_t(z)=-\partial_z^2F_t(z).
\]

Define

\[
P(t,x,y):=F_t(x+iy)F_t(x-iy)=|F_t(x+iy)|^2
\]

for real \(x,y\). Because \(P\) is even in \(y\), write

\[
R(t,x,q):=P(t,x,\sqrt q),\qquad q=y^2,
\]

using the even entire expansion rather than a branch-dependent definition.

## 1.2 Exact mixed heat/Bessel equation

A direct differentiation gives

\[
2P_t=P_{yy}-P_{xx}.
\]

Since \(P(t,x,y)=R(t,x,y^2)\),

\[
P_{yy}=2R_q+4qR_{qq}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\partial_tR
=
\partial_qR+2q\partial_q^2R-\frac12\partial_x^2R.
}
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal B_q:=\partial_q+2q\partial_q^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
(\partial_t+\tfrac12\partial_x^2)R=\mathcal B_qR.
}
\]

The operator \(\mathcal B_q\) is the squared-Bessel/BESQ(1) generator induced by ordinary one-dimensional Brownian motion after the change \(q=y^2\): for \(h(y)=g(y^2)\),

\[
\frac12h''(y)=(\mathcal B g)(y^2).
\]

## 1.3 Exact generalized-Laguerre hierarchy

Use the known expansion

\[
R(t,x,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_n(t,x)q^n,
\]

where \(L_n(t,x)=L_n(x;F_t)\) are the generalized real Laguerre expressions. Since

\[
\mathcal B_q q^{n+1}=(n+1)(2n+1)q^n,
\]

coefficient comparison yields

\[
\boxed{
\partial_tL_n+\frac12\partial_x^2L_n
=(n+1)(2n+1)L_{n+1},\qquad n\ge0.
}
\]

For \(n=0\), this reduces to the exact identity

\[
\partial_t(F_t^2)+\frac12\partial_x^2(F_t^2)
=(F_t')^2-F_tF_t''=L_1.
\]

### Correctness checks

The identity was checked symbolically on two independent backward-heat solutions:

1. \(F_t(z)=z^2-a^2-2t\). The PDE residual vanishes identically and the hierarchy holds for all nonzero coefficients \(L_0,L_1,L_2\).
2. \(F_t(z)=e^{tu^2}\cos(uz)\). The mixed PDE residual again vanishes identically.

These checks are not the proof; the proof is the direct differentiation above.

## 1.4 Semigroup intertwining / Gaussian axis transfer

Because the \(x\)-heat operator and \(\mathcal B_q\) commute,

\[
\boxed{
 e^{\frac\tau2\partial_x^2}R(t+\tau,\cdot,q)
 =e^{\tau\mathcal B_q}R(t,\cdot,q).
}
\]

Equivalently, with \(Z\sim N(0,1)\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,|F_{t+\tau}(x+\sqrt\tau Z+iy)|^2
=
\mathbb E\,|F_t(x+i(y+\sqrt\tau Z))|^2.
}
\]

This is an exact transfer of Gaussian smoothing between the real and imaginary axes.

## 1.5 Matched Gaussian moment chain

Let \(w(t,x)\ge0\) satisfy forward heat

\[
w_t=\frac12w_{xx},
\]

with sufficient decay for integration by parts, and define

\[
I_n(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}w(t,x)L_n(t,x)\,dx.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
I_n'(t)=(n+1)(2n+1)I_{n+1}(t).
}
\]

Iterating,

\[
\boxed{
I_0^{(n)}(t)=\frac{(2n)!}{2^n}I_n(t).
}
\]

This packages the generalized Laguerre hierarchy into derivatives of one Gaussian-averaged scalar observable.

## 1.6 Kernel-side dual hierarchy

For a Fourier kernel \(K_t(u)=e^{tu^2}K(u)\), define

\[
A_{n,t}(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}K_t(r+s)K_t(r-s)r^{2n}\,dr.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\partial_tA_{n,t}(s)=2s^2A_{n,t}(s)+2A_{n+1,t}(s),
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\big(e^{-2ts^2}A_{n,t}(s)\big)
=2e^{-2ts^2}A_{n+1,t}(s).
}
\]

For the Riemann kernel this interfaces directly with Csordas's associated kernels.

## 1.7 What this proves / does not prove

**Proved:** an exact structural transport law for the complete generalized-Laguerre hierarchy of any sufficiently regular real-entire backward-heat family.

**Not proved:** positivity of \(L_n(0,x)\), positive definiteness of the Riemann associated kernels, or RH.

**Novelty status:** UNESTABLISHED. A targeted literature search recovered the classical generalized-Laguerre expansion and many unrelated Laguerre/Bessel heat semigroups, but did not locate this exact coupled backward-heat/BESQ transport formula. This absence is not proof of novelty.

**Transfer value:** intrinsic to general backward-heat entire families, not specific to \(\xi\); therefore it qualifies as a genuine mathematical-tool candidate if novelty survives a deeper audit.

# 2. FALSIFIED ROUTE: persistence of the first Laguerre defect

A tempting conjecture was:

> If a real-entire backward-heat family becomes real-rooted for large positive time but is not real-rooted at an earlier time, then \(L_1=(F')^2-FF''\) must be negative somewhere at every non-real-rooted time.

This would have reduced the full Laguerre hierarchy to \(L_1\).

## Counterexample

At \(t=0\), take

\[
p(x)=x^4-3x^3-3x^2-3x-1.
\]

It has two real roots and one non-real conjugate pair (numerically approximately
\(3.96383608\), \(-0.42665673\), and \(-0.26858967\pm0.72052525i\)).

Yet

\[
L_1(x;p)=(p')^2-pp''
=4x^6-18x^5+21x^4+48x^3+30x^2+3,
\]

and its global minimum is positive (the symbolic/numerical critical-point check gives minimum \(3\)).

Under the finite backward-heat flow

\[
p_t=e^{-tD^2}p,
\]

all four roots are already real by \(t=0.5\) (and remain real for the sampled larger times).

Therefore the proposed defect-persistence theorem is false even for polynomial heat flows.

**Status:** REFUTED. Do not retry without a materially stronger Riemann-specific hypothesis.

**Lesson:** a non-real-rooted state can have \(L_1\ge0\) everywhere. The violation can migrate to higher generalized-Laguerre levels; the full hierarchy really matters.

# 3. SUBSUMED ROUTE: Vandermonde collision action

For a finite simple-real-zero heat flow obeying

\[
x_k'=2\sum_{j\ne k}\frac1{x_k-x_j},
\]

let

\[
V=\prod_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log V
=2\sum_k\left(\sum_{j\ne k}\frac1{x_k-x_j}\right)^2
=\frac12\sum_k(x_k')^2\ge0.
\]

A generic two-zero collision has gap \(d^2\asymp8(t-\Lambda)\), so the kinetic action diverges logarithmically at collision.

This is mathematically correct in the finite setting, but the Rodgers–Tao proof already develops the relevant gradient-flow Hamiltonian, inverse-square interaction energy, truncation and renormalization machinery in substantially greater depth. Therefore a bare 'collision action' is not a new route.

**Status:** SUBSUMED / DEAD END AS STATED.

A genuinely new use would require an additional renormalized no-collision theorem not already contained in Rodgers–Tao's energy framework.

# 4. CANDIDATE NEW CONSTRAINT: vertically localized symmetric Li separator

This is the strongest new constraint-first candidate from Round 1.

## 4.1 Local Möbius map

Fix a scale \(\eta>1/2\) and a height \(T\in\mathbb R\). Set

\[
a_T=\frac12-\eta+iT,\qquad
b_T=\frac12+\eta+iT,
\]

and

\[
w_{T,\eta}(s)=\frac{s-a_T}{s-b_T}.
\]

The geometric fact is exact:

\[
\boxed{|w_{T,\eta}(s)|=1\iff\Re s=\frac12.}
\]

Thus this Möbius map sends the entire critical line to the unit circle, while retaining a tunable vertical center \(T\).

## 4.2 Symmetric separator

Define, using the zero set of \(\xi\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_n(T,\eta)
:=\sum_\rho\left[
2-w_{T,\eta}(\rho)^n-w_{T,\eta}(\rho)^{-n}
\right].
}
\]

For fixed \(n,T,\eta\), the first-order terms cancel and each summand is \(O(|\rho|^{-2})\), so the sum is absolutely convergent because the xi zero set has \(\sum_\rho|\rho|^{-2}<\infty\).

The functional-equation/conjugation symmetry \(\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho\) makes \(\mathcal S_n(T,\eta)\) real.

### Soundness under RH

If RH holds, each zero lies on the critical line and \(|w|=1\). Hence

\[
2-w^n-w^{-n}=2-2\cos(n\theta)\ge0
\]

term by term, so

\[
\mathcal S_n(T,\eta)\ge0.
\]

### Local violation amplification

Suppose an off-line pair at the same ordinate is

\[
\rho_\pm=\frac12\pm\delta+i\gamma,
\qquad0<\delta<\frac12.
\]

Choose \(T=\gamma\). Since \(\eta>1/2>\delta\), the right member maps to the negative real number

\[
w(\rho_+)=-r,
\qquad
r=\frac{\eta+\delta}{\eta-\delta}>1,
\]

and the reflected member maps to \(-r^{-1}\). For even \(n\), their joint contribution is

\[
\boxed{
4-2(r^n+r^{-n})<0,
}
\]

with exponential magnitude \(\asymp-r^n\). Thus an arbitrarily small horizontal defect is exponentially amplifiable by increasing \(n\).

### Exclusion-completeness proof plan

For fixed \(T,\eta\), right-half off-line zeros have \(|w|>1\), while \(|w|\to1\) uniformly as the ordinate tends to infinity. Therefore a maximal modulus \(R>1\), if off-line zeros exist, is attained by a finite set. A simultaneous Diophantine approximation argument can choose arbitrarily large even \(n\) for which the phases of all maximal-modulus terms have positive cosine. Those terms then contribute \(-cR^n\). The non-maximal finite/medium-height terms have strictly smaller exponential rate, while the far tail is polynomially bounded because the summand is \(O(n^2/|\rho|^2)\). Hence a negative subsequence should follow.

This argument is sufficiently concrete to promote the *equivalence statement* to a proof obligation for the next certification round, but the full tail estimate and simultaneous-phase lemma should be written in theorem-proof form before promotion.

## 4.3 Analytic/prime-side representation

Let \(d=b_T-a_T=2\eta\). Define the complex-centered coefficient

\[
\lambda_n(a_T,b_T)
:=
\frac{d}{(n-1)!}
\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}
\left[(s-a_T)^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\right]
\right|_{s=b_T}.
\]

For a single zero factor, the elementary identity

\[
\frac1{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}
\big[(s-a)^{n-1}\log(s-\rho)\big]_{s=b}
=
\frac1{b-a}
\left[1-\left(\frac{\rho-a}{\rho-b}\right)^n\right]
\]

holds for every \(n\ge1\). Under the symmetric Hadamard ordering this gives the zero-sum representation, and the xi symmetry implies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_n(T,\eta)=2\Re\lambda_n(a_T,b_T).
}
\]

Leibniz also gives the finite derivative formula

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n(a,b)
=
\sum_{k=1}^n
\binom nk\frac{d^k}{(k-1)!}
(\log\xi)^{(k)}(b).
}
\]

Because \(\Re b_T=1/2+\eta>1\), the Euler product for \(\zeta\) and all differentiated logarithmic Dirichlet series are absolutely convergent there. The prime contribution collapses to

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{n,\mathrm{prime}}(T,\eta)
=-d\sum_{m\ge2}
\frac{\Lambda(m)}{m^{1/2+\eta+iT}}
L_{n-1}^{(1)}(d\log m),
}
\]

so

\[
2\Re\lambda_{n,\mathrm{prime}}
=-2d\sum_{m\ge2}
\frac{\Lambda(m)}{m^{1/2+\eta}}
\cos(T\log m)
L_{n-1}^{(1)}(d\log m).
\]

The remaining factors in \(\xi\) give explicit gamma/polynomial/archimedean terms.

This is the key reason this candidate is retained: it localizes a zero defect at height \(T\) while simultaneously producing a prime-side oscillatory expression at an absolutely convergent point \(\Re b>1\).

## 4.4 Relationship to known Li generalizations

Sekatskii's generalized Li criterion (real parameter) and the \(\tau\)-Li literature already contain broad Möbius/zero-free-region variants and arithmetic formulas. Searches also show work allowing complex evaluation parameters in derivative estimates. Therefore **novelty is not established** for the general idea of parameterized Li coefficients.

The part requiring a focused novelty audit is the simultaneous combination:

- vertical center \(T\);
- symmetric pair \(a_T=1-\bar b_T\);
- absolutely convergent separator \(2-w^n-w^{-n}\);
- explicit local exponential amplifier at the ordinate of an off-line pair;
- prime-side cosine modulation at \(\Re b>1\).

**Status:** CANDIDATE / equivalence-style tool. It is not RH progress until an independent positivity bound is proved.

## 4.5 Missing theorem that would turn it into progress

Find an unconditional structural estimate, norm representation, positive operator factorization, or prime-side inequality implying

\[
\mathcal S_n(T,\eta)\ge0

for all \(T\), all \(n\), for at least one fixed \(\eta>1/2\), **without importing RH/Li/Weil positivity in disguise**.

A weaker but potentially useful bridge theorem would show that a negative localized separator forces a violation of an already-proved pair-correlation, zero-density, heat-flow or mollified-moment bound.

# 5. STRUCTURAL DECOMPOSITION: Gram-positive covariant moment kernels

For a real square-integrable kernel \(\Phi\), let

\[
K_n(s)=\int r^{2n}\Phi(r+s)\Phi(r-s)\,dr.
\]

Define

\[
G_n(s)
:=\int (r^2-s^2)^n\Phi(r+s)\Phi(r-s)\,dr.
\]

For points \(s_i\), after a translation of the integration variable one has

\[
G_n(s_i-s_j)
=\langle T_{2s_i}(u^n\Phi(u)),
T_{2s_j}(u^n\Phi(u))\rangle.
\]

Hence every \(G_n\) is automatically positive definite (a Gram/autocorrelation kernel) for every \(\Phi\in L^2\).

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
G_n(s)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^{n-k}\binom nk s^{2(n-k)}K_k(s).
}
\]

Thus the hard Riemann kernels \(K_n\) sit in a triangular relation with a hierarchy that is positive definite for purely Hilbert-space reasons.

This does **not** prove positivity of \(K_n\), because multiplication by \(s^{2m}\) does not preserve positive definiteness. It does, however, isolate the obstruction as a multiplier/inversion problem rather than a lack of any underlying Gram structure.

**Status:** PROVED STRUCTURAL IDENTITY; likely algebraically related to standard autocorrelation identities, novelty unestablished.

**New-theory target:** find a Riemann-kernel-specific covariant basis/factorization in which the triangular inversion uses only positive-definiteness-preserving multipliers. A universal such basis cannot exist for arbitrary kernels (it would force real-rootedness universally), so any successful version must use special structure of the Jacobi/Riemann kernel.

# 6. Rejected shortcut: ordinary Pólya convexity criterion

Csordas explicitly notes that his smooth admissible associated kernels do not satisfy the elementary convexity hypothesis of Pólya's sufficient criterion. More fundamentally, a smooth even nonconstant integrable kernel has derivative zero at the origin, so it cannot be both decreasing and globally convex on \([0,\infty)\) in the way required by that criterion.

**Status:** DEAD END as a direct route.
