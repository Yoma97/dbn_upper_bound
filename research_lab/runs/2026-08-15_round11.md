## 1. Associated kernels

Let `phi_t(u)=exp(tu^2)Phi(u)` (extended evenly to the real line), and define for `n>=0`

    K_(n,t)(r)
      = int_R phi_t(s+r) phi_t(s-r) s^(2n) ds.

For the cosine transform `H_t`, the standard associated-kernel identity is

    L_n(x;H_t)
      = [2*2^(2n)/(2n)!]
        int_R K_(n,t)(r) cos(2xr) dr.

In particular,

    L_1 = 4 C[K_1](2x),
    L_2 = (4/3) C[K_2](2x),

where `C[f](xi)=int_R f(r)cos(xi r)dr` for even real `f`.

# 2. PROVED: Paired Associated-Kernel Criterion

Define the single convolution kernel

    C_t = K_(1,t) * K_(2,t).

Under the standard integrability/continuity hypotheses satisfied by the Riemann kernel,

    Fourier(C_t)(xi)
      = Fourier(K_(1,t))(xi) Fourier(K_(2,t))(xi).

Because the kernels are even,

    L_1(x;H_t)L_2(x;H_t)
      = (4/3) Fourier(C_t)(2x)

with the Fourier convention `Fourier(f)(xi)=int f(r)e^(-ixi r)dr`.

Hence by Bochner/Mathias,

    L_1(x;H_t)L_2(x;H_t) >= 0 for all real x

if and only if

    C_t = K_(1,t)*K_(2,t)

is positive definite.

Combining with Round 10:

    RH

is equivalent to

    K_(1,t)*K_(2,t) is positive definite

for every `0<t<=1/2`.

### Logical warning

This is still an RH-equivalent dynamic criterion. Its value is compression: the static Csordas characterization asks for positive definiteness of the entire associated-kernel hierarchy, whereas heat dynamics reduces the obstruction to one paired convolution built only from `K_1` and `K_2`.

# 3. DESTROYER: ordinary convexity/log-concavity is not enough

A first attempt would be to prove positive definiteness of `C_t` by a simple Polya convexity criterion. This fails structurally: `C_t` is an even smooth positive convolution and typically has a smooth maximum at the origin, incompatible with convexity on `(0,infinity)` unless it is locally flat in a degenerate way.

More decisively, Csordas gives the admissible-type example

    phi(x)=exp(-x^2)(15+x^2+x^4)

and notes that `log phi` is strictly concave, while its Fourier transform is, up to a positive constant,

    F(x)=exp(-x^2/4)(260-16x^2+x^4),

which has non-real zeros.

Direct differentiation gives

    exp(x^2/2)L_1
      = [x^8-24x^6+712x^4-13536x^2+84240]/2,

    exp(x^2/2)L_2
      = [x^8-16x^6+696x^4-18496x^2+107088]/8.

For example at `x=3`,

    L_1>0,  L_2<0.

Thus `L_1L_2<0` there. Strict log-concavity of the original Fourier kernel therefore does **not** imply the paired-kernel positivity required by the dynamic criterion.

Any successful kernel property must be genuinely stronger/different than ordinary log-concavity, but should remain weaker than an endpoint `PF_infinity`/full-Laguerre condition if it is to represent new leverage.

# 4. PROVED: logarithmic-curvature / KdV form

Away from a zero of a real entire function `F`, set

    u = partial_x log F,
    v = -partial_x^2 log F.

Then

    L_1/F^2 = v.

A direct logarithmic-derivative calculation yields

    L_2/F^2
      = [6v^2-v_xx]/12.

Indeed, with `u=(log F)'`,

    F'/F=u,
    F''/F=u'+u^2,
    F'''/F=u''+3uu'+u^3,
    F''''/F=u'''+4uu''+3(u')^2+6u^2u'+u^4,

and

    L_2
      = F F''''/12 - F'F'''/3 + (F'')^2/4
      = F^2[(u')^2/2+u'''/12]
      = F^2[6v^2-v_xx]/12.

Therefore

    L_1L_2
      = F^4 v(6v^2-v_xx)/12.

The Round-10 criterion becomes, away from zeros and by continuous extension in the polynomial Laguerre form,

    v(6v^2-v_xx) >= 0.

Define

    KdVDef[v] := 6v^2-v_xx.

Then the condition is simply

    v * KdVDef[v] >= 0.

The equality `v_xx=6v^2` is the zero-constant stationary-KdV/Weierstrass local equation; the single-pole potential `v=(x-a)^(-2)` satisfies it exactly. This is a structural bridge/analogy, not a claim that the Riemann problem has been reduced to a known KdV theorem.

# 5. Root-coordinate interpretation for polynomials

If

    F(x)=c product_j (x-z_j)

(counting multiplicities and ignoring a harmless exponential factor), then

    v(x)=sum_j 1/(x-z_j)^2.

Consequently

    v_xx=6 sum_j 1/(x-z_j)^4,

and

    [6v^2-v_xx]/12
      = sum_(i<j)
          1/[(x-z_i)^2(x-z_j)^2].

Thus

    L_1/F^2 = e_1(a_1,...,a_m),
    L_2/F^2 = e_2(a_1,...,a_m),

with `a_j=(x-z_j)^(-2)`.

For a real-rooted configuration every `a_j` is positive on the real axis, so both elementary symmetric functions are nonnegative. A heat collision with complex roots is detected when the first two symmetric curvatures acquire opposite signs, exactly as in Round 10.

# 6. Burgers/Calogero side

For a heat family `partial_tF=-F_xx`, the logarithmic slope satisfies

    u_t + u_xx + 2u u_x = 0,

and the curvature `v=-u_x` obeys

    v_t = -v_xx + 2v^2 - 2u v_x.

Thus the two-Laguerre obstruction is a sign cone for a Riccati/Burgers curvature evolving under a drift-reaction backward-parabolic equation. At the zero level this is compatible with the familiar Calogero-type zero interaction law.

This identifies another possible route: construct an invariant Lorentz/Krein cone for `(v,KdVDef[v])` using special structure of the Riemann initial kernel. No such invariant cone is proved here.

# 7. New lower-level research problem

The endpoint statement

    C_t positive definite for every t>0

is equivalent to RH in the Riemann family. Therefore it is not itself the desired missing lemma.

The desired theorem must have the form

    StructuralProperty(phi_t)
       =>  K_(1,t)*K_(2,t) positive definite,

where `StructuralProperty`:

1. is formulated entirely on the Fourier kernel side;
2. is independently checkable without knowing zeros of `H_t`;
3. holds for a natural class wider than the Riemann kernel;
4. is not equivalent to full total positivity / all generalized Laguerre inequalities;
5. survives counterexample testing against known strictly log-concave kernels whose Fourier transforms have nonreal zeros.

Promising but unproved candidates include a finite-order matrix total-positivity/variation-diminishing condition and a two-component Krein-positive transfer structure coupling the first two associated kernels.
