# 1. PROVED: completeness and resolution cost of the vertically localized Möbius separator

Fix

\[
c=\tfrac12,\qquad a=c-\eta+iT,\qquad b=c+\eta+iT,\qquad \eta>\tfrac12,
\]

and

\[
w_{T,\eta}(s)=\frac{s-a}{s-b}.
\]

For \(\rho=c+\delta+i\gamma\), putting \(y=\gamma-T\),

\[
\boxed{
|w_{T,\eta}(\rho)|^2
=\frac{(\eta+\delta)^2+y^2}{(\eta-\delta)^2+y^2}.
}
\]

Hence \(|w|=1\) exactly on the critical line, \(|w|>1\) to its right and \(|w|<1\) to its left. The functional-equation/conjugation partner \(\rho^*=1-\bar\rho\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{w(\rho^*)=\frac1{\overline{w(\rho)}}.}
\]

At the matched height \(T=\gamma\),

\[
\log |w(\rho)|
=\log\frac{\eta+\delta}{\eta-\delta}
=2\operatorname{artanh}(\delta/\eta)
\sim \frac{2\delta}{\eta}.
\]

Therefore detecting a horizontal displacement \(\delta\) by powers \(w^n\) intrinsically requires degree scale

\[
 n\asymp \frac{\eta}{2\delta}
\]

when \(\delta\) is small. Since an Euler-product anchor uses \(\eta>1/2\), arbitrarily small off-line displacements cannot be resolved at uniformly bounded degree.

### Completeness mechanism

Assume the usual symmetric zero multiset and sufficient second-order summability, e.g.

\[
\sum_\rho (1+|\rho|)^{-2}<\infty.
\]

If an off-line zero exists, then for a fixed suitable \(T\) there is a maximal modulus

\[
R=\max_{\Re\rho>1/2}|w_{T,\eta}(\rho)|>1.
\]

The maximum is achieved by finitely many zeros because \(|w(\rho)|\to1\) as \(|\Im\rho|\to\infty\). By simultaneous recurrence/Diophantine approximation of their phases, there exists an infinite subsequence of even integers \(n_j\) on which their leading contributions align. Thus any symmetric power-sum separator of the Round-1 type has a negative contribution of order \(-cR^{n_j}\), while all strictly smaller-modulus terms are lower exponential order and the tail is controlled by the second-order summability.

**Conclusion:** any global nonnegative or merely subexponential-lower-bound theorem for the complete localized separator family would already exclude every off-line zero. It is therefore endpoint-strength in substance. This route is retained as a quantitative microscope, not promoted as the desired intermediate theorem.

**Status:** PROVED INTERNAL PROPOSITION; NOVELTY NOT CLAIMED.

# 2. PROVED: Gram–Laguerre jet triangular identity

Let \(\phi\) be an even real kernel with sufficient decay and

\[
F(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(u)e^{iux}\,du.
\]

Define the Csordas-type moment kernels

\[
K_n(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}\phi(r+s)\phi(r-s)r^{2n}\,dr
\]

and the Gram covariants

\[
G_n(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}(r^2-s^2)^n\phi(r+s)\phi(r-s)\,dr.
\]

Expanding \((r^2-s^2)^n\) gives

\[
\boxed{
G_n(s)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^{n-k}\binom nk s^{2(n-k)}K_k(s).
}
\]

With \(u=r+s\), \(v=r-s\), one has \(r^2-s^2=uv\), and direct Fourier transformation yields

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R}G_n(s)e^{2ixs}\,ds
=\frac12|F^{(n)}(x)|^2.
}
\]

Using the standard generalized Laguerre normalization

\[
L_k(x;F)=\frac{2\,2^{2k}}{(2k)!}\int K_k(s)\cos(2xs)\,ds,
\]

one obtains the exact triangular identity

\[
\boxed{
4^n|F^{(n)}(x)|^2
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk(2k)!\,\partial_x^{2(n-k)}L_k(x;F).
}
\]

Binomial inversion gives

\[
\boxed{
(2n)!L_n(x;F)
=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^{n-k}\binom nk4^k\partial_x^{2(n-k)}|F^{(k)}(x)|^2.
}
\]

If \(E_k(x)=|F^{(k)}(x)|^2\) and \(S E_k=E_{k+1}\), this is compactly

\[
\boxed{
(2n)!L_n=[(4S-\partial_x^2)^nE]_0.
}
\]

This identifies the complete Laguerre hierarchy as repeated action of a single jet operator on a manifestly nonnegative energy sequence. The operator is not known to preserve the relevant positive cone; that is the nontrivial missing structure.

**Status:** PROVED INTERNAL IDENTITY; NOVELTY PENDING.

# 3. PROVED: master-kernel decomposition and the anti-Gaussian obstruction

Define, for \(\alpha\ge0\) in the domain of convergence,

\[
M_\alpha(s)=\int e^{\alpha r^2}\phi(r+s)\phi(r-s)\,dr,
\]

and renormalize

\[
N_\alpha(s)=e^{-\alpha s^2}M_\alpha(s).
\]

Then

\[
N_\alpha(s)
=\int e^{\alpha(r^2-s^2)}\phi(r+s)\phi(r-s)\,dr
=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{\alpha^m}{m!}G_m(s).
\]

Every \(G_m\) is an autocorrelation/Gram kernel and hence positive definite. Therefore

\[
\boxed{N_\alpha\ \text{is positive definite for }\alpha\ge0,}
\]

and its \(\alpha\)-derivatives lie in the positive-definite cone coefficientwise.

But

\[
M_\alpha(s)=e^{+\alpha s^2}N_\alpha(s),
\]

and multiplication by \(e^{+\alpha s^2}\) is not a positive-definiteness preserving multiplier. The target moment kernels \(K_n\) are precisely the Taylor coefficients/derivatives of this unrenormalized family at \(\alpha=0\).

Thus the obstruction is isolated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Gram/Hilbert positivity is easy; the anti-Gaussian localization factor is the positivity-destroying step.}
}
\]

This suggests a general theory of **anti-Gaussian positive-definite robustness**, but such a definition is useful only if one proves nontrivial sufficient conditions that apply beyond the Riemann kernel. Merely defining the robustness radius to be the endpoint where all \(K_n\) remain positive definite would be tautological.

**Status:** PROVED STRUCTURAL DECOMPOSITION; THEORY CANDIDATE.

# 4. OBSTRUCTION IDENTIFIED: phase localization barrier

For global/Gaussian averages centered at the origin, positivity inherited from a positive Fourier kernel is structurally accessible. At a general localization center \(x_0\), Fourier double integrals acquire the oscillatory factor

\[
e^{i(u+v)x_0},
\]

and cancellation reappears. This explains why global/averaged positivity can coexist with failure of the pointwise Laguerre inequalities needed for Laguerre–Pólya membership.

The obstacle is therefore not simply “prove the kernel is positive”; it is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{upgrade global Gram positivity to uniformly localized positivity in the presence of uncontrolled phase cancellation.}
}
\]

A viable new theorem must control this phase without inserting RH-equivalent information.

**Status:** RESEARCH OBSTRUCTION / DESIGN PRINCIPLE.

# 5. DEPRIORITIZED: direct localized-Li positivity or subexponential bound

The completeness result in §1 shows that proving, for the actual zeta zeros, a bound strong enough to prohibit exponential negativity of every localized separator already excludes every off-line zero. Such a statement may be a valid criterion, but without an independently provable lower-level mechanism it is another endpoint formulation.

**Decision:** retain the separator as a microscope for candidate structures and cancellation mechanisms; do not treat its positivity/growth bound as the missing intermediate theorem.

# 6. CURRENT SPECTRAL FRONTIER

The 2025 Connes–Consani–Moscovici finite-prime spectral construction produces finite-dimensional/selfadjoint approximants whose associated entire functions have only real zeros under their stated simplicity/evenness hypotheses. Their paper explicitly states that numerical spectra approach the Riemann zeros and that a rigorous convergence proof would establish RH; convergence of suitably normalized regularized determinants toward \(\Xi\) is presented as the missing analytic direction (arXiv:2511.22755).

Connes–van Suijlekom (arXiv:2511.23257) prove a general convolution/quadratic-form mechanism: under lower-bounded selfadjointness and a simple isolated extremal eigenfunction, the Fourier transform of that eigenfunction has only real zeros. This supplies a reusable real-rooted approximant mechanism but not the limiting identification with \(\Xi\).

Suzuki (arXiv:2606.09096, current preprint) gives a screw-function/selfadjoint-operator framework for localized Weil forms without assuming RH and formulates an operator-limit conjecture.

The common missing feature is therefore **limit control**, not finite-level selfadjointness.

# 7. PROVED GENERAL LEMMA: normal-family closure of real-rooted approximants

### Lemma (classical compactness closure, packaged for the spectral program)

Let \(E_j\) be entire functions such that:

1. every zero of every \(E_j\) is real;
2. \(\{E_j\}\) is locally bounded on \(\mathbb C\) (hence a normal family by Montel);
3. there is a subset \(U\subset\mathbb C\) having an accumulation point in \(\mathbb C\) such that
   \[
   E_j(z)\to \Xi(z)\quad(z\in U).
   \]

Then

\[
\boxed{E_j\to\Xi\quad\text{locally uniformly on }\mathbb C,}
\]

and every zero of \(\Xi\) is real.

### Proof

By local boundedness, every subsequence has a locally uniformly convergent subsubsequence \(E_{j_k}\to E\) with \(E\) entire. On \(U\), the pointwise anchor convergence forces \(E=\Xi\); the identity theorem gives equality everywhere. Hence every convergent subsubsequence has the same limit \(\Xi\), so the full sequence converges locally uniformly to \(\Xi\). If \(\Xi\) had a nonreal zero \(z_0\), choose a small disk around \(z_0\) disjoint from the real axis. Hurwitz's theorem would force zeros of \(E_j\) in that disk for all sufficiently large \(j\), contradicting real-rootedness. ∎

This lemma is classical analysis, not claimed new. Its value is that it identifies what **does not need to be proved**: one need not track individual zeros or prove spectral convergence zero-by-zero.

# 8. HIGHEST-PRIORITY NEW MISSING THEOREM

## Spectral Normal-Family / Uniform-Growth Theorem — candidate

Let \(E_{N,\lambda}\) denote a canonically normalized entire function arising from the finite-prime selfadjoint spectral construction (for example the normalized regularized determinant/Fourier-transform object after removing explicit elementary factors).

The minimal desired theorem is to establish, along a cofinal regime \((N,\lambda)\to\infty\):

### A. Uniform local growth / compactness
For every \(R>0\),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{N,\lambda}\sup_{|z|\le R}|E_{N,\lambda}(z)|<\infty
}
\]

after the correct canonical normalization, or a quantitatively equivalent bound giving a normal family.

### B. Anchor identification
On a set with an accumulation point (ideally a half-plane/strip where the prime-side representation is absolutely controlled), prove

\[
\boxed{E_{N,\lambda}(z)\to\Xi(z).}
\]

### C. Finite-level real-rootedness
Verify uniformly along the chosen cofinal regime the hypotheses ensuring every \(E_{N,\lambda}\) is real-rooted (in particular the finite-level simplicity/evenness conditions required by the current spectral theorem, unless replaced by a stronger general theorem that removes them).

Then the lemma in §7 gives RH immediately.

### Why this is a better intermediate target

It is not a positivity criterion equivalent to RH stated directly in terms of the zeros. It is a compactness/growth theorem about a concrete family of finite-prime entire functions/operators. The same theorem would be useful for spectral approximations of other completed L-functions and for real-rooted limits of selfadjoint finite-dimensional models.

### Main danger / anti-circularity test

A bound obtained by inserting prior knowledge that \(\Xi\) has only real zeros, or by assuming convergence of the target spectrum itself, is circular. The required estimates must come from operator norms, determinant bounds, trace-class/Schatten estimates, finite-prime Euler products, or another independent analytic mechanism.

**Status:** HIGHEST-PRIORITY CANDIDATE; NOT PROVED.

# 9. SECONDARY CANDIDATE: first-crossing spectral no-go

For localized Weil quadratic forms on nested windows, let \(A_a\) be the associated lower-bounded selfadjoint operator and \(\lambda_0(a)\) its ground-state energy, with domains identified through the natural inclusion/extension-by-zero formalism where valid.

The variational principle suggests a nonincreasing ground-state function as the window grows. A structural proof route would be:

1. establish continuity (preferably norm-resolvent/form convergence) of \(A_a\) in \(a\);
2. assume a first threshold \(a_*\) with \(\lambda_0(a_*)=0\);
3. derive the exact zero-mode equation;
4. prove, from the prime/archimedean decomposition of the Weil form, that this equation has no nonzero admissible solution.

The explicit finite-window Weil form contains an archimedean term and a finite sum of bounded selfadjoint prime-shift/correlation operators. This makes a first-crossing analysis concrete, but no zero-mode exclusion theorem is currently established here.

A naive argument based only on rational independence of \(\log p\) is insufficient: finite-interval delay/shift equations can possess nontrivial solutions.

**Status:** CANDIDATE; LOWER PRIORITY THAN §8.

# 10. Smallest proof obligations for Round 3

1. **Define the exact normalized spectral approximant \(E_{N,\lambda}\)** from arXiv:2511.22755 without ambiguity and record its determinant/product formula.
2. **Derive operator-theoretic local growth bounds** for \(E_{N,\lambda}\) from finite-rank/rank-one perturbation determinant estimates, avoiding spectral convergence assumptions.
3. **Seek a weak anchor set**: determine whether prime-side formulas yield convergence at even one complex neighborhood or another infinite set with a finite accumulation point after normalization.
4. **Audit finite-level real-rootedness hypotheses** (simplicity/evenness of the lowest state) and either prove them along a cofinal regime or replace them by a version of the Connes–van Suijlekom theorem robust to multiplicity.
5. In parallel, develop the anti-Gaussian PD-robustness theory only if a general sufficient theorem can be proved for non-Riemann kernels.

The preferred Round-3 breakthrough is therefore not “prove RH” and not “prove Weil positivity”; it is a genuinely independent estimate of the form

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{(N,\lambda)\in\mathcal C}|E_{N,\lambda}(z)|\le C_R
\quad(|z|\le R)
}
\]

together with an anchor identification on a nontrivial set. That would convert a currently numerical spectral convergence phenomenon into a compactness theorem and make the critical-line conclusion a forced consequence of classical complex analysis.
