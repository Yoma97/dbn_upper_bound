# Round 31 — Every finite even-completed theta block develops a high-frequency Laguerre defect

**Date:** 2026-08-16

**RH status:** OPEN.

**Status labels:** PROVED / STRUCTURAL NO-GO / MODULAR-CANCELLATION NECESSITY / NOVELTY UNVERIFIED.

## 0. Executive verdict

Round 30 proved that the raw theta-mode diagonal/off-diagonal split is not a finite decomposition. A natural repair is to complete each mode, or a finite block of modes, by reflection at the origin so that every block becomes individually integrable on the full line.

This round proves that **every nonempty finite theta block repaired in this way necessarily fails the first Laguerre inequality at sufficiently high Fourier frequency.**

The reason is universal. Let `A(u)` be a smooth rapidly decaying function on `[0,infinity)` and form the even completion

\[
B(u)=A(|u|).
\]

If the first nonzero odd derivative of `A` at the origin has order `2k+1`, then the cosine transform

\[
F(x)=2\int_0^\infty A(u)\cos(xu)\,du
\]

has a power tail

\[
F(x)=C x^{-p}+O(x^{-p-2}),
\qquad p=2k+2,
\qquad C\ne0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
(F')^2-FF''
=-pC^2x^{-2p-2}+O(x^{-2p-4})<0
}
\]

for all sufficiently large `x`.

For a finite nonempty set `S` of Riemann theta modes,

\[
A_S(u)=\sum_{n\in S}a_n(u),
\]

`A_S` cannot be even. Hence at least one odd derivative at zero is nonzero. Consequently the even completion

\[
B_S(u)=A_S(|u|)
\]

always produces a negative high-frequency `L_1` tail.

Thus no finite blockwise completion can provide positive-definite first associated kernels. A viable theta decomposition must be genuinely infinite/modular and must cancel **all odd boundary jets simultaneously**.

## 1. General endpoint-jet lemma — PROVED

Let `A` be `C^infinity` on `[0,infinity)` and suppose `A` and all its derivatives decay faster than every inverse power at infinity. Define

\[
F(x)=2\int_0^\infty A(u)\cos(xu)\,du.
\]

Assume

\[
A'(0)=A^{(3)}(0)=\cdots=A^{(2k-1)}(0)=0,
\]

but

\[
A^{(2k+1)}(0)\ne0.
\]

Repeated integration by parts gives

\[
\boxed{
F(x)
=2(-1)^{k+1}A^{(2k+1)}(0)x^{-2k-2}
+O(x^{-2k-4}).
}
\]

Set

\[
p:=2k+2,
\qquad
C:=2(-1)^{k+1}A^{(2k+1)}(0)\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
F(x)=Cx^{-p}+O(x^{-p-2}),
\]

\[
F'(x)=-pCx^{-p-1}+O(x^{-p-3}),
\]

and

\[
F''(x)=p(p+1)Cx^{-p-2}+O(x^{-p-4}).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
(F')^2-FF''
&=p^2C^2x^{-2p-2}
-p(p+1)C^2x^{-2p-2}
+O(x^{-2p-4})\\
&=\boxed{-pC^2x^{-2p-2}+O(x^{-2p-4})}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(F')^2-FF''<0
}
\]

for sufficiently large real `|x|`.

This is a purely Fourier-endpoint statement and uses no RH information.

## 2. Interpretation for even completion

If

\[
B(u)=A(|u|),
\]

then `B` is even but fails to be `C^{2k+2}` at the origin precisely when `A^{(2k+1)}(0)` is the first nonzero odd jet.

The algebraic Fourier tail generated by this cusp is fatal to `L_1` positivity.

Therefore an even completion can have a chance of satisfying

\[
L_1(F)(x)\ge0\quad\text{for all real }x
\]

only if

\[
\boxed{
A^{(2j+1)}(0)=0
\quad\text{for every }j\ge0.
}
\]

For analytic `A`, this means the germ of `A` at zero is already even.

## 3. Application to one raw Riemann theta mode

Recall

\[
a_n(u)=\pi n^2(2\pi n^2e^{4u}-3)
\exp(5u-\pi n^2e^{4u}).
\]

Writing

\[
y=\pi n^2e^{4u},
\]

we obtain

\[
\frac{a_n'(u)}{a_n(u)}
=5-4y+\frac{8y}{2y-3}
=\frac{-8y^2+30y-15}{2y-3}.
\]

At `u=0`, `y=pi n^2`. The numerator vanishes only when

\[
y=\frac{15\pm\sqrt{105}}8,
\]

and no value `pi n^2` with integer `n>=1` equals either root. Hence

\[
\boxed{a_n'(0)\ne0\quad\text{for every }n\ge1.}
\]

Thus the simplest even completion

\[
b_n(u)=a_n(|u|)
\]

has `k=0`, `p=2`, and its Fourier transform satisfies

\[
\widehat b_n(x)=Cx^{-2}+O(x^{-4}),
\]

with `C!=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
L_1(\widehat b_n)(x)
=-2C^2x^{-6}+O(x^{-8})<0
}
\]

for all sufficiently large `x`.

So **every individual reflected theta mode fails the first Laguerre inequality**.

## 4. No nonempty finite theta block is analytically even — PROVED

Let `S` be a finite nonempty set of positive integers and define

\[
A_S(u)=\sum_{n\in S}a_n(u).
\]

We claim `A_S` is not even.

As `u->-infinity`, for fixed `n`,

\[
\pi n^2e^{4u}\to0,
\]

and therefore

\[
a_n(u)
=-3\pi n^2e^{5u}+O_n(e^{9u}).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
A_S(u)
=-3\pi\left(\sum_{n\in S}n^2\right)e^{5u}
+O_S(e^{9u})<0
}
\]

for sufficiently negative `u`.

On the other hand, as `u->+infinity`, every mode in the finite set has

\[
2\pi n^2e^{4u}-3>0
\]

and hence

\[
A_S(u)>0
\]

for sufficiently large positive `u`; moreover it decays super-exponentially there.

If `A_S` were even, then `A_S(u)=A_S(-u)` for large positive `u`, contradicting the opposite signs and incompatible tail scales above.

Thus

\[
\boxed{A_S\text{ is not even for every finite nonempty }S.}
\]

Because `A_S` is analytic, not all odd derivatives at zero can vanish. Therefore there exists a least `k>=0` such that

\[
A_S^{(2k+1)}(0)\ne0.
\]

## 5. Every finite reflected block has negative high-frequency L1 — PROVED

Define the individually finite even completion

\[
B_S(u)=A_S(|u|).
\]

Let

\[
F_S(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}B_S(u)e^{-ixu}\,du
=2\int_0^\infty A_S(u)\cos(xu)\,du.
\]

By Section 4 there is a least nonzero odd jet `2k+1`, and by Section 1

\[
F_S(x)=Cx^{-2k-2}+O(x^{-2k-4}),
\qquad C\ne0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
L_1(F_S)(x)<0
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }|x|.
}
\]

Equivalently, the first associated kernel of the completed finite block cannot be positive definite.

This proves that **finite block grouping does not repair Round 30**.

## 6. What the full Riemann kernel does differently

The full Riemann theta kernel

\[
\Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n(u)
\]

is even and `C^infinity`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^{(2j+1)}(0)=0
\quad(j=0,1,2,\ldots).
}
\]

These are infinitely many exact cancellation identities among the raw theta modes.

In particular, already at first order,

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}a_n'(0)=0
\]

in the correctly justified theta sense: the positive contribution from the first modes is cancelled by the rest. Higher odd jets satisfy analogous modular cancellations.

Thus smooth evenness of the Riemann kernel is not a modewise fact; it is an **infinite modular cancellation law**.

Round 31 shows that this infinite cancellation is not cosmetic. Without it, the first Laguerre expression is forced negative at high frequency.

## 7. Consequence for modular/Ewald decompositions

Any decomposition

\[
\Phi=\Phi^{(1)}+\cdots+\Phi^{(M)}
\]

intended to prove `K_1` positive definite termwise must satisfy, for every proposed component whose associated kernel is to be positive definite,

\[
(\Phi^{(j)})^{(2q+1)}(0)=0
\quad\text{for all }q\ge0,
\]

or else its Fourier transform has an eventually negative `L_1` tail.

Hence a successful finite-number-of-blocks decomposition must use **infinite theta resummations inside each block** so that every block is separately smooth and even.

A finite collection of raw modes, even after reflection, is ruled out.

This is substantially narrower than the original `theta decomposition` idea.
